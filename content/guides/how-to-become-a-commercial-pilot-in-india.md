Becoming an airline pilot in India takes between two and four years for most self-funded candidates. The licence that lets you earn money flying is the Commercial Pilot Licence (CPL), issued by the Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA). This guide walks through the full route from a first medical check to a First Officer seat with an airline, based on DGCA's own rules.

Rules and fees change. Every requirement in this guide is taken from DGCA's published Civil Aviation Requirements (CAR). Confirm the current version at [dgca.gov.in](https://www.dgca.gov.in) and ask flying schools or airlines directly for fees.

## The short version

| Step | What it is | Typical time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Class 2 medical | Fitness check to begin training | 1 day |
| Student Pilot Licence (SPL) | Lets you fly with an instructor | 1–3 months |
| Private Pilot Licence (PPL) | 40–50 hours; solo and basic cross-country | 6–9 months |
| Class 1 medical | Stricter medical before CPL | 1 day |
| Commercial Pilot Licence (CPL) | 200 flight hours total, DGCA ground exams | 18–24 months |
| Multi-engine and Instrument ratings | Needed to fly airliners | 1–2 months |
| Type rating on an airliner (A320, 737, ATR) | Specific aircraft training | 2–3 months |
| First Officer with an airline | Line training, then regular flying | — |

## Who can apply

The DGCA sets the eligibility rules. The main ones:

- **Age:** 17 for the SPL, 18 for the CPL. There is no upper age limit for the licence, but most airlines prefer candidates under 32 at joining.
- **Education:** completed 10+2 with physics and mathematics. If you did not take these subjects, you can clear them through the National Institute of Open Schooling (NIOS).
- **Medical:** DGCA Class 2 at the start, DGCA Class 1 before the CPL. Both are done at DGCA-approved examination centres. Common issues that need checking first: eyesight (glasses are allowed within limits), colour vision, blood pressure, and ear or sinus problems.
- **Language:** English for radio communication, assessed to ICAO level 4 or above.

## Pick a route: India or abroad

Most Indian candidates train either at a DGCA-approved flying school in India or at a school abroad (the United States, Canada, South Africa, the Philippines and New Zealand are common choices). Each has trade-offs.

**India.** Training happens on your home licence, so there is no conversion step. Weather, aircraft availability and instructor shortages can stretch the course beyond its stated length. Costs are usually ₹35–55 lakh for the CPL. DGCA publishes the list of approved [Flying Training Organisations](https://www.dgca.gov.in).

**Abroad.** Training is often faster because of better weather and more aircraft, and total cost at a well-chosen school can be similar to India. You then convert the foreign licence to a DGCA CPL, which means Indian ground exams, an Indian skill test and extra flight hours in India. Budget three to six months and ₹8–15 lakh for the conversion.

## The DGCA ground exams

For the CPL, DGCA tests you on six papers: Air Navigation, Aviation Meteorology, Air Regulations, Technical General, Technical Specific (for the aircraft type you fly) and Radio Telephony (handled by the Ministry of Communications' WPC Wing). You can sit the exams while you build flight hours. Pass marks and syllabus are on the [DGCA Pariksha](https://pariksha.dgca.gov.in) portal.

## Flight hours

The DGCA CPL needs 200 hours of flight time, including minimums for solo flight, cross-country, night flying and instrument time. On top of the CPL, airlines ask for a Multi-Engine Instrument Rating (MEIR), which adds about 15 hours of multi-engine flying and the instrument skill test.

## The type rating

Airliners need a type rating specific to the aircraft. In India this is usually the Airbus A320, Boeing 737 or ATR 72. A type rating costs ₹25–35 lakh if you pay yourself, or is included in some airline cadet programmes. The rating runs for about two months and ends with a simulator check and base training on the aircraft.

## Cadet programmes

Several Indian airlines run cadet schemes with partner training organisations. These schemes screen candidates up front, then put them through PPL, CPL, ratings and type rating in one managed programme. Fees are similar to self-funded training, but the airline job at the end is secured if you pass each stage. Watch the airline's own careers page for openings; cadet intakes are irregular. ## The money

CPL training fees are not set by DGCA and are not published in one place. Each flying training organisation (FTO) sets its own fees, and the total depends on how long you take to finish, exchange rates for training abroad, and which type rating you add on top.

**Before you commit money**, ask each FTO you shortlist for:

- A written fee schedule covering every stage (PPL, hour-building, CPL, MEIR, exam fees, licence fees)
- Payment in instalments tied to training stages, not a single lump sum up front
- How many hours of flying are included in the quoted fee, and what the hourly rate is after that
- How long the last three batches actually took to finish (quoted times are usually optimistic)

Type ratings on specific airliners (A320, 737, ATR 72) are a separate cost, done either at your expense or as part of an airline cadet programme. Each airline cadet programme publishes its own fee or loan structure on its careers page.

Education loans for pilot training are available from most Indian public-sector banks, usually against collateral. Ask for a loan sanction letter before you pay the school.

## What airlines look for after the CPL

A DGCA CPL alone is not a job offer. Indian airlines also look at:

- Total hours (minimums vary, often 200 hours CPL + MEIR)
- Pass record in DGCA papers (fewer re-sits is better)
- Communication and attitude in the interview and simulator check
- No gaps in training; a steady run from PPL to CPL in two or three years looks better than a six-year gap-filled journey

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Paying the full school fee up front.** Pay in instalments tied to training progress wherever you can.
- **Choosing a school on price alone.** Check aircraft utilisation, instructor turnover and how long the last three batches took to finish.
- **Skipping the Class 1 medical** before you commit money. If a condition is going to stop you, it will stop you now, not after you spend ₹20 lakh.
- **Falling for cadet "guaranteed job" scams.** No agent can promise an airline job. {{ link('guides/spot-fake-airline-job-offers') }} covers the warning signs.

## Once you have the CPL

With the CPL, MEIR and a type rating, you can apply to airlines as a First Officer. Hiring is cyclical - it depends on fleet orders and pilot retirements. If airlines are not hiring in your window, options include flight instruction (adds hours and keeps you current), general aviation and charter flying, or working abroad where your DGCA CPL can be converted to a local licence.
