Gulf carriers hire thousands of cabin crew every year, and the three big ones - Emirates, Qatar Airways and Etihad - follow a similar recruitment pattern. This guide covers what each airline asks for, how an open day works, and the mistakes that end most applications before they get to a first interview.

Requirements change. Always check the airline's own careers page before you apply, and never pay anyone for a referral or an "application fee" - these airlines recruit directly.

## The common requirements

Most Gulf cabin crew jobs ask for the same core things:

- **Age:** 21 or over at application. There is no upper age limit stated, but recruitment favours candidates in their 20s and early 30s.
- **Reach:** arm reach of around 212 cm on tiptoes. This matters more than raw height because crew need to close overhead lockers. Many airlines no longer publish a minimum height for that reason, but a reach of 212 cm usually matches a height of about 160 cm.
- **Education:** completed secondary school (10+2 in India, or equivalent).
- **English:** fluent, written and spoken. A second language is a strong advantage.
- **Health:** physically fit, able to swim at least 25 metres unaided, no visible tattoos in uniform (short-sleeved shirt and skirt), good vision (glasses or contact lenses are allowed within limits).
- **Documents:** valid passport with at least 12 months remaining, no criminal record, ability to live and work in the UAE or Qatar.
- **Experience:** at least one year in customer service, hospitality, retail or similar. Previous flying is not required - all three airlines train from scratch.

## How recruitment runs

All three carriers run **open days** in cities around the world. The format varies but usually looks like:

1. **CV drop.** You hand your CV and a passport-size photo to a recruiter. Dress in business attire exactly as the airline shows on its site - tailored dark skirt or trousers, a neat blouse or shirt, closed-toe heels for women, dark suit and tie for men. Hair tied back. Minimal, natural makeup. Nails trimmed and clean. First impressions decide more than most candidates realise.
2. **Short group exercise.** Those who pass the CV screen stay for a group task - introducing a partner, discussing a scenario, or role-playing a customer service situation. Recruiters watch for how you listen, include others and stay calm.
3. **Reach test.** You will be asked to touch a mark on the wall at 212 cm on tiptoes. Flat feet, not jumping.
4. **English assessment.** Short written test or group discussion.
5. **Final interview.** One-to-one with a recruiter, usually at a later date for candidates who pass the open day. Questions are competency-based: tell us about a time you handled a difficult customer, a safety mistake you spotted, a cultural difference you adapted to.
6. **Medical and background checks.** Full medical in Dubai, Doha or Abu Dhabi, then joining.

The whole process from first open day to joining takes two to six months.

## What ends most applications

Based on the published guidance from each airline's own careers page, the recurring issues at open days are:

- **Grooming off.** Visible tattoos (even small ones on wrists or ankles), chipped nail polish, loose hair, strong perfume or cologne, or an outfit that does not match the airline's grooming guide.
- **CV too long.** One page, clean layout, dates aligned, no photo collage. See {{ link('guides/aviation-cv-that-gets-past-hiring-systems') }} for the format.
- **Group exercise.** Candidates who talk over others or go silent. The task is designed to see how you behave in a team; the "right answer" matters less than how you reach it.
- **Weight-to-height.** All three airlines require crew to look well in uniform without visible strain. The number is not published; use common sense.
- **English.** Not perfect grammar, but clear pronunciation, confident answers and the ability to follow a safety briefing word for word.

## About scams

Cabin crew recruitment is a frequent target for fake agents. **No Gulf airline charges a fee to apply, and no legitimate agent needs to "submit your CV for you".** Open day venues and dates are only on the airline's own careers site. If someone asks for money for a reference, a shortlist spot, or a visa before you have a signed offer letter from the airline, walk away. ## What the life is actually like

Gulf cabin crew work from a base in the UAE or Qatar. Rosters mix short-haul (3–7 hour sectors to the Middle East, Europe, India, Africa) with long-haul (12+ hours to the Americas and Asia-Pacific). A typical month is 80–100 flying hours spread across 15–18 duty days, with the rest off. Accommodation in shared company flats is standard for the first contract; most crew move to their own place after a year or two.

Standby duties and quick turnarounds mean sleep patterns shift constantly. The first six months are the hardest, especially for crew who have not lived away from home before. The upside is paid international travel, tax-free salary in Dubai, Doha and Abu Dhabi, and a role that stays interesting because every flight is different.
